Open questions

  1. Can learners Try again after Check answer? Each try would be an attempt and an FSRS rating, and the list would show the latest result.
  2. When does a question leave the mistake queue? Once its latest check gets full marks, or never until Reset, with FSRS only changing the order?
  3. Should bank topics with mistakes also appear in Learning's Review tab, beside the workspaces?
  4. /bank needs sign-in today. Should signed-out visitors get it with the read-only runner and the public header?
  5. The copy dialog's primary button says Copy, not Save, and it lists only workspaces the learner can edit. Fine?
  6. The chapter picks where a new quiz goes and narrows the quiz list. Should quizzes from other chapters still be listed?
  7. The type filter shows on IELTS topics only, since HKDSE questions have no types. Use short labels like True/False/Not given instead of the official long names, and list only types the topic has?

1. Answering on /bank

  • Check answer grades on the server, because learners never receive the answer key. It also records the attempt and its FSRS rating.
  • A checked question looks like quiz review, with Your answer and Correct answer tags, part marks and a collapsed worked solution.
  • Right means full marks, the same rule as the quiz result squares. Signed-out visitors keep today's read-only runner with no Check answer.
1A

Button under each question, mark leading each row

  • Check answer, and Try again once checked, sit under the question on the right.
  • Each list row starts with a mark. A check is right, a cross is wrong and an empty circle is not answered yet, as in the bank mock approved on 25 Sep.
  • One line under the topic name counts right answers and questions to retry.

    In the figure, A, B and C are points on a circle. The straight line TA touches the circle at A, and CB produced meets TA at T. It is given that ∠BAT = x and ∠ATB = y.
    0 / 1
    1. Find ∠BAC in terms of x and y.
      Worked solution
      ∠ACB = ∠BAT = x (∠ in alt. segment)
      ∠ABC = ∠BAT + ∠ATB = x + y (ext. ∠ of △ABT)
      ∠BAC = 180° − ∠ACB − ∠ABC = 180° − x − (x + y) = 180° − 2x − y (∠ sum of △ABC)
      The answer is D.
      A (180° − x − 2y) pairs the tangent with the wrong angle, taking ∠ACB = y. B (180° − x − y) is ∠TAC from △ATC, before ∠BAT is taken away. C (180° − 2x + y) subtracts instead of adds in the exterior angle, taking ∠ABC = x − y.

2. Mistake review per topic

  • The queue holds this learner's missed questions in one topic, lowest FSRS retrievability first, 20 per session. Questions come one at a time with Check, then Next. No workspace is involved.
  • Ratings follow the workspace review rule. A score below 0.5 is Again, below 0.7 is Hard, anything else is Good. The end screen is the workspace one.

3. Copy to quiz

  • Copying uses the chat's copy route, so every copied question keeps its own credits and shows them under the question in the quiz.
  • Copying one question means selecting one. There is no separate single-copy action.

  1. 1.
    14 marks
    The Music of Trinidad's Carnival
    ATrinidad is a small island nation in the Caribbean, just off the coast of Venezuela. It is home to one of the world's largest carnivals. The city carnivals of the New World grew out of celebrations held before Lent in medieval and Renaissance Europe. At these times, large numbers of people took to the streets to have fun. They put on comic shows that mocked people in power and often challenged the social order. French, Spanish and Portuguese settlers brought these Catholic customs to the New World. There, they mixed with the traditions of African slaves and their descendants. The result was spectacular new celebrations in cities such as Rio de Janeiro in Brazil, Port of Spain in Trinidad, and New Orleans. Over time, these festivals took on a more African character. African mask traditions became central to them. So did new music styles with African roots. These featured music made up on the spot, dance rhythms with accents off the main beat, and call-and-response singing, in which a leader sings a line and a group answers.

      City carnivals in the Americas have their origins in festivals that took place in Europe before Lent.
