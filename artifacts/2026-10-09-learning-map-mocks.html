  • Biomes in this workspace (seeded from its id): city (dense) → town (medium) → farms (medium) → country (sparse) → woods (medium) → river (sparse) → industry (dense) → river (sparse).
  • Biomes are 260 to 440 map units long, about 4 to 7 items each, blending over 60 units at each border (the bank uses 420 to 720, about 7 to 12 questions).
  • Stations replace the bank's START sign and keep buildings out of their own outline only. Names wrap to two lines of at most 18 characters; longer ones end in an ellipsis (see World history below).
  • The density budget is split between the ground above the line (56%) and below it (44%), roughly by their height, so city and industry are built up on both sides. In front, blocks, towers, warehouses and tanks may stand partly below the bottom edge. Scenery may come close to the line: the line, stops and numbers are drawn on top (numbers with a halo).

CTrail colour from the cover

The trail, stops and flag take the workspace's cover colour; links keep the theme's accent. The seven cover colours all read on the light card as they are. Paper covers are the only light ones (cream, white, grey, yellow paper), so their trail uses the paper's ink instead: the blue ink of lined and grid paper, the brown line of genkō paper. In dark mode every colour is mixed 38% towards white so it stays readable on the dark card.

BBiomes on their own

Each biome drawn alone, with the label measured from it: mean visual weight per question-width over 20 seeds (box area × how much of it is ink or colour, water and fields × 1.6). Under 7 is sparse, under 16 medium, above that dense. Two of the bank's rules carry over (no dense next to dense, no three sparse in a row, a dense one at least every fifth here, since the only dense biomes are urban), plus one new one: the urban level (city and industry 3, town 2, farms 1, countryside and woods 0, river any) changes by at most one across a border, so a city always thins out through town or a river. When both can't hold, the dense-every-fifth rule gives way.
