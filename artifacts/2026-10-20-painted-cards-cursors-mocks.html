Painted cards and collaboration cursors

Capy Notebook UI mocks, 2026-10-20. Two looks from landing-page mock B "Painted": thick painted frames and backgrounds on cards and pages, and the "Study together" cursors. Screens are the real app (MSW data) with each option injected into the live page. Desktop is 1440 px, phone is 360 px.

Cards: Dashboard

Workspace cards and file cards. Today is the current app for comparison.

AFrame

Every card becomes a white card inside a 9 px painted frame (outer radius 20, inner 12), as in the landing mock's "What Capy makes" fan cards. Text stays in normal ink, so nothing depends on the paint for contrast. The thumbnail well stays as it is.

BFill and well

Workspace cards are painted edge to edge, replacing today's pattern covers, with white text over a light ink wash (the landing mock's bento tiles). File cards stay white; their grey thumbnail well becomes paint, and the note, page or slide preview sits on it as a white page. A file without a preview shows its icon on a white tile.

Where the colour comes from

S1Workspace cover colour

A file card takes its workspace's scene, so a workspace's files share its colour (the Dashboard shots above: every Biology 101 file is Meadow). This keeps lists calm.

S2File kind

The scene follows the kind: Word Sea, PowerPoint Sunset, sheets Wheat, Markdown Mist, notes Meadow, quizzes Sea, flashcards Dusk (the landing mock's fan used kinds like this). Lists from many workspaces look more varied.

Pages

HDashboard greeting on paint

PShared pages in a painted frame

Public note, quiz and flashcard pages: the grey margin around the page becomes a 16 px painted frame (8 px on phones). These are the pages people outside Capy see, so they carry the landing site's look.

Not drawn, possible later: empty states (no workspaces, an empty workspace), Learning's Continue review header, quiz and review result summaries. Not proposed: the app shell behind the sidebar (its text would sit on paint), editors, dialogs and panel lists.

Dark mode

The same images under a 42% ink wash (a CSS gradient layer over the image), so dark mode needs no extra files. The inner card and the white preview pages follow the theme's surface.

Cost: live SVG filters against images

The landing mock paints with inline SVG filters (turbulence, displacement, a 38 px blur, grain). Measured on a 24-card grid, hovering 9 cards (shadow change, as Card does today) and scrolling, headless Chromium on the Mac:

Raster work, whole runLongest frame on hoverBytes
Plain cards (no paint)0.2 s18 ms0
Pre-rendered WebP (background-image)0.6 s18 ms7 scenes, about 32 KB at 800 px (17 KB at 480 px), cached once
Live SVG filters5.7 s53 ms (12 dropped frames)about 6 KB of SVG inline

Every repaint of a card re-runs the filters, so the hover shadow alone drops frames, and phones raster more slowly. Inline SVGs also share one id space: in a test sheet every card took the first card's gradient until the ids were made unique. Recommendation: render the scenes once at build time (a script next to src/lib/coverArt.ts) to WebP. AVIF saved only 5% on these soft images and decodes more slowly, so WebP it is. Cards use background-image with background-size: cover; dark mode adds the wash layer.

2. Collaboration cursors

Today a co-editor shows as a bare 2 px bar in a colour hashed onto the whole hue wheel, so yellows and cyans nearly vanish on white, and nothing says who it is. Every option below draws on the caret the editor already renders: the bar and the flag are pseudo-elements of that one empty leaf, so no DOM is added to the text and no extra React render happens. Selections get a 20% tint of the peer's colour (13% today).

C1Name flag, always shown

2 px caret; flag just above it: 11 px bold, 6 px radius with the square corner where it meets the caret, white text on the peer colour. This is the landing mock's "Study together" scene.

C2Flag while active, then a small head

The flag shows while the peer moves or types and for 3 s after, then folds into a 6 px head on top of the caret. BetterOffice's Word editor uses the same 3 s. A screen with several idle peers stays readable.

Live, C2's timing: Leo types; Mina moves every few seconds, so her flag comes back and folds again.

The cell is the basic unit of life. Today we covered structure, transport, and energy for a refresher.

C3C2 plus pointers

Peers' mouse pointers with pill name tags, as drifting in the landing hero. It needs one more awareness field (pointer position, at most 20 updates a second per peer, through the server like carets), positions stored against the block under the pointer so they land right at any window width, and an overlay outside the editable. Useful on whiteboards later; in a note the caret already shows where people are.

Office files

BetterOffice draws its own presence overlays: in Word, a 2 px caret and a flag (11 px, weight 600, corners 4 4 4 0) that fades after 3 s; in PowerPoint and Excel, shape or cell outlines with name labels. They use inline styles and three different 8-colour lists. Capy passes no name, colour or presence into the Office runtime today (officeCollaboration.ts sends only a random client id), so Office files show no collaborators yet.

The editor session's presence branch (verified names, the header avatar group) is wiring presence into Office files. After it lands, the three BetterOffice flags would move to this spec (6 px radius, bold, the shared palette) on capy-ci, through that session. This change stays in notes.
