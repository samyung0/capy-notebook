Picked: F2 (the whole window painted, the sidebar a white card), with the page's notch removed and the top bar's items moved into the sidebar. Cursor flag size Z2.

Cursors: the editor session has the final C2/Z2 spec (caret, flag, fade, right-edge flip, palette, screen readers). It builds cursors for notes and every Office format in a follow-up branch after its header/presence branch lands. C3 pointers are left out (section 4 has the write-up).

1. No notch: the top bar moves into the sidebar

From 1280 px the page loses its notch and the top inset bar goes. The sidebar gets the layout it already has below 1280 px: Search and Notifications under the app name, and the profile row at its foot. That row now opens the profile menu (Settings, Billing & usage, Theme, Log out) instead of linking to Settings, so its log-out icon goes. Page headers get the full width.

Phones are unchanged (avatar top right). The workspace page has no app sidebar and keeps its own bar above the side panel; it isn't changed here.

2. What Arc's theme picker does

Arc is closed source. The Browser Company said in 2025 it might open-source or sell it but has not, partly because Arc shares its internal toolkit with their new browser, Dia. What the controls do, from an open-source clone of the editor and from user guides:

ControlWhat it does
Dotted field and the dotsA colour picker: left to right is hue, top to bottom is saturation. Brightness is not editable. Each dot is one colour.
+ and −Add a second or third colour (three at most); remove the selected one. One colour gives a flat wash; two or three give a soft diagonal gradient. These are the colour stops you guessed.
Swatch rowPreset colours; a click puts that colour on the selected dot.
Wavy sliderIntensity: how strongly the colours tint the sidebar and window.
Round dialGrain: a fixed noise tile laid over the gradient, from none to a lot. It is static, not animated.
Sparkle, sun, moonAppearance: automatic, light or dark. Capy already has Theme (Latte, Frappé, Mocha) in the same drawer.

How it is drawn

Arc-style custom colours rule out one pre-rendered image per scene, so every frame is drawn the same way: one 4 KB grey brush texture (the clouds and streaks from round 1, in grey) is laid over a gradient of the chosen colours with an overlay blend, plus a small grain tile. Presets are just colour sets through the same path, so there are no per-scene images. When the choice changes, the browser bakes the result once into an image (a canvas, about 10 ms), and the page then pays only an image's cost. Measured over 20 window resizes at 1440 by 900:

Full-window backgroundRaster work, 20 resizes
Baked image95 ms
Texture and gradient blended live382 ms
Live SVG filters (the landing page)3,459 ms

Dark themes darken the result with the same ink wash as before. The saved choice is small: a preset name, or up to three colours plus strength and grain.

4. C3, mouse pointers (open from round 2)

No pointer is sent today: a note's presence carries the name, colour and caret or selection, at most once per 50 ms. Pointers inside a file are possible (anchored to the block under the mouse, drawn outside the editable, one mapping per Office format). Pointers over side panels have no shared meaning, because everyone's panels show different things; presence per item (avatars on file-tree rows) would need a workspace-wide presence channel. Viewers get no presence at all. Suggestion: skip pointers for now.
