Learning · proposal · 22 Oct 2026

Study plans

A study plan is your own ordered list of what to study, picked from chapters, files and whole workspaces across every workspace you can read. Progress on each step comes from the read marks, attempts and ratings we already record, so a plan adds no new tracking. A workspace only shows the plan steps that fall inside it, plus a link to the step after.

What I propose for the first version

  • Steps are chapters, single files or materials, or a whole workspace. A step is done when every tracked item in it is done, the same count as the Study tab's "4 of 5". New items added later reopen the step.
  • The current step is the first step not done, so steps can be finished out of order and Continue never gets stuck.
  • No calendar and no dates yet. Order is the plan. The one date I'd consider later is an optional "Finish by" per plan with a pace line (option C), never a day-by-day schedule. ROADMAP already parks the calendar until the notebook loop has weekly users.
  • No AI in the first version. A new plan starts from reading order and you drag rows to reorder. Later, workspace chat can read the steps inside its own workspace, and a planner on Learning could see titles and progress counts only, never content (see AI scope below).
  • Private to you, like progress. Shared plans for studying with friends come after, and the data model leaves room for them.

Both per user, per workspace. Share is on by default. Flipping it changes what you see straight away and never rewrites anything, because every write is kept both ways (below).

What Share progress changes

  • On: read marks, done counts, Continue, the map and the Study page rows count everything you did in this workspace, under any plan.
  • Off: they count only what you did while the active plan was active. Switching plans then shows that plan's own marks.
  • Review follows the same switch. Recall (FSRS), the miss weighting from recent ratings and the suggested-review modes (Tricky, Fading, Just learned) read all ratings when on, and only the active plan's when off. That covers Quick review, Review on the Study page and this workspace's suggestions on Learning's Review tab.
  • All results never filters by plan. Every quiz and flashcard attempt stays listed whatever the switches say.
  • Learning counts per workspace by its own switch. A plan spanning two workspaces can count shared progress in one and plan-only progress in the other.

How records link to plans

  • Logs carry a plan id. attempts, review_log, review_sessions and review_answers each get plan_id, the active plan at write time (null = the workspace's own plan). Yes, the attempt row itself links to the plan. It is a plain column, not a foreign key, so deleting a plan leaves its results counted in shared progress and in All results, and never makes them look like the workspace plan's.
  • States are kept twice. study_progress (read, started, not tracked) and review_states (FSRS) gain a scope: shared, workspace, or a plan id. Every write updates the shared row and the active plan's row in one transaction, so both are always current and the switch only picks which to read. Each row is updated from its own previous state (FSRS state per plan comes from that plan's ratings only).
  • Mark as unread becomes a row with no state instead of a delete, so "latest write wins" holds per scope.
  • One read helper, progressScope(user, workspace) → shared or the active plan's scope, used by every progress and review read: StudySummary, quickReview, scoreItems, the review overview, Learning progress, the chat agent's read_study_progress. AllResults and GET /api/review/sessions don't call it.
  • Deleting a plan deletes its scope rows. Reset progress clears every scope in the workspace; attempts and the log stay, as today.

Your answers, turned into rules:

  • Each workspace has an active plan, the workspace's own plan by default. You change it with the plan switch on the Study page. Pressing Continue on a plan in Learning also makes it active in that workspace, so "the plan you last worked on" and "the active plan" are the same thing.

With the workspace's own plan active it reads plain "Continue".

  • Hide study progress is one switch per user per workspace revised, in the Study page's ⋮. It does what Track progress off does today: marks, the read toggle, scores and Continue states disappear in that workspace, Learning's Review skips it and chat can't read its progress, while results keep being saved. Turning it back on shows everything.
  • The same switch is the Hide on Learning revised. Today a workspace with progress off already drops off Learning, so instead of a second "hide" the Learning row's ⋮ flips this switch. "Show all" at the right of the Workspaces heading brings hidden ones back at half opacity. Workspace plans can't be deleted; your own plans can.
  • A new workspace plan starts with nothing done and every file tracked, as today: no row means untouched, and untouched counts as tracked until you stop tracking an item.
  • The Study side panel goes away. Its Continue, Review and Quick review move to the top of the centre Study page (today's Study progress page), which becomes the one place to study a workspace.

What "written to the active plan" means decided in round 4: a Share progress switch

It can mean the plan owns the progress (per plan), or the plan is a label on a shared record (shared). Concretely: you read Sample spaces.pdf while Winter exams is active, then switch Statistics 201 back to its own plan. Per plan, the file shows unread again. Shared, it shows read in both, and the record says it was read during Winter exams.

Shared (I'd pick this). A read mark or a quiz result is a fact about you and the file, so it counts in every plan that has the file. Each record carries the plan it was made under, so a plan can list its own results, but done counts agree everywhere. A new plan over chapters you've already read starts with them done.

Per plan. Each plan keeps its own read marks and done counts, so a "second pass before finals" plan starts empty. But the same file then shows read in one plan and unread in another, and the workspace row on Learning can say 0 of 18 while your Winter exams plan shows 9 of them done. That is the two-things confusion again. FSRS memory would stay per person in both cases, since what you remember doesn't depend on the plan.

If the second-pass case matters, I'd add a "Start this plan fresh" action later (it would mark its items unread) rather than splitting progress.

Round 2 · 22 Oct · A picked

One concept: every workspace is already a plan

Today each workspace has an order (chapters, then their files, then Others), done marks, a Continue and a "4 of 5" per chapter. That is a plan in all but name. So instead of adding plans next to progress, split the two cleanly:

  • Progress is the facts per item: read, started, not tracked, attempts, ratings. One store, unchanged.
  • A plan is an order over those facts: steps that expand to items. Every step count, Continue and map is computed the same way for any plan.
  • Each workspace has a built-in plan made from its chapters, always in step with the Files order. It is never stored and can't be reordered on its own, because then the workspace would have two orders. Anyone who can read the workspace sees the same steps, with their own marks.
  • Your own plans are built from workspace plans. A chapter step is one step of a workspace plan; a whole-workspace step is the workspace plan itself and stays live as chapters are added. The add popover is just "pick steps from workspace plans".
  • To study a workspace in another order, make your own plan with its chapters. The built-in one keeps following Files.

What this changes from round 1: no separate Plan tab. Learning's Progress tab already lists workspaces (in progress, finished), so your plans join that list and the lead plan is drawn as list A. The workspace Study tab's chapter list is the workspace plan, so it only needs one row saying which of your plans include it.

What it costs

  • Workspace plans need no table: they are today's inReadingOrder grouped by chapter. Only your own plans are stored (study_plans, study_plan_steps from section 6).
  • GET /api/learning/progress gains plans next to the workspace rows, and lead can name a plan. Its "last studied" is the latest progress write on any of its items, so no new column.
  • The railway map takes steps instead of chapters (a station per step). For a workspace plan that's exactly today's map.
  • One renderer for steps on Progress, the Study progress page and the Study tab's Done list.

New questions

  1. Two Continues in one workspaceInside Statistics 201, Continue follows the workspace plan; on Learning, Winter exams' Continue follows yours. They agree unless your plan reorders that workspace's chapters. I'd keep it that way: the workspace never changes behaviour because of a plan you made elsewhere.
  2. Workspaces only reached through a planKeep their own row under Workspaces (shown above), or hide them while a plan covers them. I'd keep them; hiding makes rows appear and vanish as plans change.
  3. "New plan from this workspace"Copies chapters as separate steps so you can reorder them; chapters added later don't join that copy. Fine, or skip the action and only offer Add to a plan?
  4. NamingCall the built-in one "Workspace plan" in the UI, or never say "plan" for it and only show the workspace name. I'd show the name only and keep "plan" for the ones you make.

5 · AI

What the AI may see

The chat agent's tools are scoped to one workspace (source.read on that workspace). A plan spans workspaces, so it can't simply be handed to chat. Three levels, in the order I'd build them:

  • v1, none. A new plan starts empty; the popover adds chapters in reading order. No model call.
  • v2, workspace chat reads its own steps. read_study_progress gains the plan steps inside that workspace and whether this workspace is the current step. It never sees titles from other workspaces, so "after this, Alkanes in Organic Chemistry" stays out of the model's context. Read only; chat can't change a plan.
  • Later, a planner on Learning. Its own agent with one tool that lists workspaces you can read with chapter titles, item counts and progress counts, never content or search. Useful for "split these into 6 weeks". It needs its own permission check per workspace and its own metering, which is why it waits.
6 · Data

Two tables, no new progress

TableColumnsNotes
study_plansid, user_id, name, created_at, updated_atPer user, private, not charged to storage. Cap 10 per user. A later finish_by column only if C is picked.
study_plan_stepsplan_id, position, workspace_id, chapter_id?, file_id?, material_id?At most one of the three set; none = whole workspace. Cascades with its workspace, chapter, file or material. Cap 50 per plan. Unique per plan and target.
  • GET /api/learning/plans returns plans with steps, each step's done and total (from study_progress, skipping trashed items) and the current step's next item. Steps in workspaces you can no longer read come back with no title.
  • PUT /api/learning/plans/{id} replaces name and step order in one write; drag sends the whole order.
  • GET /api/workspaces/{id}/study gains plans: plans with steps in this workspace, those steps, and the step after (title and workspace name, checked for read access).
  • Workspaces with progress off: steps stay, counts show "Progress off" and never count as done.
7 · Your call

Decisions

  1. Plan viewA list, B route line on top of the list, or both. I'd pick A.
  2. DatesNone now, or the optional Finish by of C. I'd ship without dates and revisit once people use plans.
  3. Where it livesNew Plan tab on Learning, or a section at the top of Progress. I'd pick the tab; Progress stays the map.
  4. One plan or severalSeveral with a dropdown (cap 10), or one per user. Several: an exam plan and a language plan run side by side.
  5. Step unitsChapter, single file or material, whole workspace. Also question-bank topics? I'd leave bank topics out of v1; the bank has its own map.
  6. Study tabS1 plan block or S2 one row. I'd pick S1.
  7. ContinueLearning's Continue follows the plan; the workspace's Continue keeps reading order. Or should the workspace's Continue also jump to its own current plan step?
  8. Review inside a planNo review steps; suggested reviews stay on the Review tab. Or add "Review chapter" steps that finish on a session?
  9. AIv1 none, v2 workspace chat reads its own steps, planner later. OK?
  10. SharingPrivate now. Later a plan could be shared with friends, each seeing the others' step marks, with locked steps for workspaces they can't open.
