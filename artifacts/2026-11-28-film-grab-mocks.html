Grabbable film objects

The scene's author marks objects as grabbable (interactive(triangle, move=True, rotate=True, label="Drag or turn the triangle")). In the player (option D), grabbing one pauses the film; it stays where it was left until the film plays on, then goes back to where the scene has it. Only the grabbed object moves: the base and height lines here are separate objects and stay put, since making them follow needs the scene's own logic.

Try it: play the film, grab the triangle or (from slide 3) the top vertex. Drag to move, Shift-drag to turn (two fingers on a phone). Below: how grabbable objects are shown (G2 picked), turning a 3D view, then a Brilliant-style check for later.

G1

Shown on hover

Nothing marks a grabbable object until the pointer is over it: then a dashed outline, the author's label and a grab cursor. The cleanest film; on a phone, nothing tells you until you touch it.

G2

Cued when it stops

While the film plays nothing shows. When it is paused, or waiting at the end of a slide, grabbable objects get a faint, slow halo (Desmos' halo); the author's label shows on hover, as in G1.

3D

Turning a 3D view

Any 3D scene can be turned, with nothing tagged: drag the empty film to orbit the camera, wheel or pinch to zoom. The scene keeps its own animation under the viewer's angle; the title and the formula are fixed to the frame and stay put. An object tagged rotate=True (the sphere, on slide 3) spins on its own when dragged instead. Like grabbing, it pauses the film; Reset view, Play, Next or a seek put the scene's own camera back.

Later

A Brilliant-style check

The author adds a target to a slide: interactive(vertex, move=True, target=dot, label="…"). When the film waits at that slide, a task row under the film asks for it; Check answers at once, and a wrong try keeps the object where it is so the viewer can adjust. Exporter and page work; the engine part is the same pose and bounds.
