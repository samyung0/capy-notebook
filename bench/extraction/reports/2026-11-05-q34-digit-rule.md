# Make a quiz: a digit rule for 2022 P2 Q34 (2026-11-05)

Epo asked whether one more rule fixes Q34's lost zero. The printed number has six zeros, and the page
read that goes in as indexed text has five. Script: `scripts/copy_first.py`, variants `-q34` and
`-q34batch`, with `-armb` or `-armc`. No app code changed.

## Verdict

**Neither rule clearly helps, so I would not add one.** C reads six zeros in 4 of 10 alone, against 1 of
10 with no rule, but only 1 of 5 in the real batch. It also costs more reasoning.

| Arm | Flow | Six zeros | Printed exponents | Billed | Billed a run | Wall s | Copy reasoning, mean (range) | Copy output, mean (range) |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A, no change | Alone | **1 of 10** | 10 of 10 | $0.0321 | $0.0032 | 23 (20-30) | 274 (96-747) | 528 (339-1,014) |
| B, Epo's line | Alone | **2 of 10** | 10 of 10 | $0.0375 | $0.0038 | 30 (19-61) | 1,103 (107-5,518) | 1,436 (359-5,770) |
| C, also names the page image | Alone | **4 of 10** | 10 of 10 | $0.0449 | $0.0045 | 39 (20-84) | 2,532 (0-8,552) | 2,828 (268-8,819) |
| C | Real batch, Q33 to Q45 | **1 of 5** | 5 of 5 | $0.0308 | $0.0062 | 52 (39-72) | 2,156 (706-3,212) | 5,521 (4,004-6,853) |
| No rule, for comparison | Real batch, saved `mapcopy16` runs | **0 of 4** | 4 of 4 | | | | 1,171 to 5,434 | |

Every other run read five zeros, so no run read any other count. The cached share on the copy call was
0.995 or more throughout.

- **Noise:** 1, 2 and 4 of 10 are not separated at this size. Even A against C has about a 30% chance of
  arising by luck. 1 of 5 against 0 of 4 says nothing either way.
- **The likely cost is real:** a rule raises reasoning on a call meant to copy, up to 8,552 tokens for one
  question. That is the low-reasoning problem the last report listed, made worse.

## Is one question a fair stand-in?

**Not quite.** Alone, the call spends all its attention on one question, and C's reasoning there (2,532 tokens)
is more than the whole batch's (2,156 for 13 questions). That is the likely reason C reads better alone than
in the batch. So the batch row is the one to trust, and it has only 5 runs.

## The arms

The flow is X over the whole selection, then one copy call at `{"effort": "low"}`, each run with its own
nonce. Alone, the ask is `Copy question 34 now.` In the batch it is `Copy questions 33 to 45 now.` The system
prompt is the last report's, plus at most one line right after Copying's first bullet:

```text
A: nothing added.
B: - Important: count the digits carefully for long numbers.
C: - Important: count the digits carefully for long numbers, on the page image, since the indexed text may drop or add digits.
```

Epo's test said to run the winning arm in the real batch only if it clearly helped. C did not clearly help. I
ran it anyway, because it was the only way to answer whether the one-question test is a fair stand-in, and it
cost $0.03.

## What it can't show

One digit string on one paper. Nothing here tests whether a rule helps other long numbers, or harms other
questions. The source of the wrong digit is the page read, and I did not test sending less of it.
