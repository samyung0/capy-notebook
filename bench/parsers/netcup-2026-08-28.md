# Netcup parser VM benchmark — 2026-08-28

This run used the dedicated 8-vCPU, 15 GiB Debian VM and the CPU-only parser
image. The image was 2.68 GB. Warm idle parser memory was about 3.0 GB cgroup
usage. No swap was configured or used.

The fixtures are deterministic canaries, not a substitute for a representative
set of real user documents. The HTML reports remain local/generated artifacts
under `bench/parsers/results/` and are intentionally ignored by Git.

## Accuracy and mode comparison

| Input | Mode | Wall | OCR pages | Output chars | Decision |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| 40-page native PDF | Marker only | 16.86 s | 0/40 | 39,097 | pass |
| 40-page native PDF | Selective | 16.56 s | 0/40 | 39,097 | pass |
| 40-page native PDF | All-page OCR | 37.65 s | 40/40 | 39,097 | reject: 127% slower, no recovery |
| 40 native + 2 scan pages | Marker only | 17.48 s | 0/42 | 39,097 | reject: scan canaries missing |
| 40 native + 2 scan pages | Selective | 22.58 s | 2/42 | 44,723 | pass |
| 40 native + 2 scan pages | All-page OCR | 41.56 s | 42/42 | 44,723 | reject: 84% slower, no recovery |

The mixed-format smoke corpus also passed selective and all-page OCR for a
one-page DOCX, two-page PPTX, one-page XLSX, two-page scanned PDF, and direct
JPEG input. Visual inspection confirmed the canary text, repeated scan rows,
tables, and returned bounding boxes. Marker-only correctly failed the scanned
PDF and passed the native Office conversions.

The production default is therefore `selective_rapidocr`. The selector is
deliberately generous: it OCRs weak, malformed, image-heavy, and sparse text
layers. All-page OCR remains available as an explicit retry and benchmark mode.

## OCR concurrency sweep

The two-page scan was submitted in bursts while the parser admitted at most two
OCR-heavy jobs:

| Submitted jobs | Burst | Throughput | Median job | Max parser memory |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 3.81 s | 0.525 pages/s | 3.81 s | 4.69 GiB |
| 2 | 8.92 s | 0.449 pages/s | 8.48 s | 4.83 GiB |
| 4 | 17.70 s | 0.452 pages/s | 13.10 s | 5.47 GiB |
| 6 | 25.46 s | 0.471 pages/s | 16.54 s | 5.45 GiB |
| 8 | 34.69 s | 0.461 pages/s | 22.62 s | 5.44 GiB |

There were no errors and memory plateaued below 6 GiB, but throughput did not
improve as more work was submitted. Keep eight outer workers for queueing and
non-parse ingest stages, while retaining the two-job OCR admission limit. Revisit
that limit only with real traffic and host samples; this benchmark gives no
evidence that raising it helps.
