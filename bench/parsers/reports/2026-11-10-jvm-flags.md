# Parser: the JVM with the quick compiler alone

2026-11-10 and 2026-11-11. Java (OpenDataLoader, one JVM a PDF) is about 60%
of a parse's CPU, and a profile on a Mac showed more than half of that is the
JVM compiling its own code. Two flags were measured on the ingest host, in the
parser image and its sandbox: `-XX:TieredStopAtLevel=1` (the quick compiler
alone) and `-XX:+UseSerialGC`.

Epo first decided on both (2026-11-10, after the Java-only run). The
whole-parse run the next day showed the serial collector far slower at the
1 GiB heap on a heavy book, and Epo decided on the quick compiler alone
(2026-11-11, `parser/odl/java.py`). The collector and the heap stay as they
were.

## Answers

- **The output is the same.** In the Java-only runs each of the 80 PDFs gave
  one JSON under every flag set in every run (1,280 JVM runs, no error), and so
  did the 1,400-page cap fixture and the heavy 1,400-page book.
- **On ordinary documents the quick compiler alone halves the Java time under
  load.** Seven Java calls at a time over the 80 PDFs: 98 and 93 s with the
  JVM's defaults, 51 and 48 s with the flag, for 58% less JVM CPU. One at a
  time on two CPUs, the UAT and local parser today, the set takes 11% less
  time for 39% less JVM CPU.
- **Whole parses of ordinary documents, with the stage speedups, take less
  time for 30% to 39% less CPU.** The 80 PDFs through the parser, main
  against the speedups with the flag: one at a time on 8 CPUs 634 to 589 s
  summed (7% less), one at a time on 2 CPUs 685 to 571 s (17% less), and
  seven in flight on 8 CPUs the set done in 184 s against 127 to 129 s (30%
  less). The parsed content of all 80 bundles, compared from the 8-CPU
  one-at-a-time passes, is byte-identical.
- **On the heavy book it cuts the Java phase by about a third under load, and
  the queue takes as long.** Seven parsing together: 30 to 32 s for 39 to
  44 s. Java is about a seventh of that book's parse, and eight books at
  seven children finished in 571 s for 566 s.
- **A long PDF alone on eight idle CPUs is slower.** Of the 25 PDFs of 209
  pages or more, 19 took longer; the 15 over 300 pages took 20% longer
  together. The 1,016-page book's Java call went from 8.0 to 10.4 s and the
  heavy 1,400-page book's from 14 to 21 s. The optimizing compiler had been
  running on cores nothing else used.
- **The serial collector was dropped.** It saved memory (the largest JVM over
  the 80 PDFs 1.07 to 0.85 GiB) for 11% less JVM CPU in the Java-only run. On
  the heavy 1,400-page book at the 1 GiB heap it took the Java call alone
  from 14 to 36 s, and to 42 s with the quick compiler too. Under seven-way
  load both flags gave no gain on that book (44 s).

## What ran

**Java-only run (2026-11-10).**

- **Host and image.** The ingest host (AMD EPYC 9645, 8 vCPU), the parser
  image at main `2c6b02156` (OpenJDK 17.0.21), in a throwaway container with
  the parser's hardening (read-only root, no capabilities,
  `no-new-privileges`, the HOME tmpfs, 512 pids) and no network.
- **The Java call.** A script calls `odl.java.run` as a parse child does, a
  fresh process and JVM for each PDF: the sandbox, `-Xmx1g`,
  `MALLOC_ARENA_MAX=2` and the CPU count from the container's quota, with a
  flag set put after the executable. Font repair, flattening and the repair
  stages did not run, so these are not whole-parse times.
  `scripts/jvm_flags.py` is that script tidied for an image that already has
  a flag; it has not run on the host in that form.
- **Pages.** 80 PDFs, 11,341 pages: papers, reports, worksheets, Office
  exports and books. 13 have 1 to 5 pages, 32 have 6 to 50, 20 have 51 to 300
  and 15 have more, up to 1,016. The 21 that font repair or flattening rewrites
  went in rewritten, as Java reads them. The 1,400-page fixture is the parser
  budget's `limit-1400p`, as built, twice per flag set and shape.
- **Shapes.** Seven JVMs at a time on 8 CPUs, as production's seven parse
  children (two rounds, the second in reverse flag order, one container a
  flag set); one at a time on 8 CPUs; one at a time on 2 CPUs, as the nonprod
  parser. Alone, the four flag sets ran side by side on each PDF, once.

**Whole-parse run (2026-11-11).**

- **Harness.** `scripts/capacity_pass.sh`, as the
  [v19 stress run](2026-10-21-parser-v19-sandbox-stress.md): a throwaway
  parser container in production's shape (14 GiB, 8 CPUs, seven parse
  children, depth 8), driven through the artifact route.
- **Input.** That run's heavy 1,400-page book (a figure-heavy textbook
  repeated to the page cap), eight copies sent together: seven parse and the
  eighth waits a round.
- **Images.** The live parser image at main `9150a9867`, and thin layers over
  it: main with the quick compiler alone, and four with the stage speedups of
  `claude/parser-speed` (`21fe0b41f`, not on main at the time): the speedups
  alone (twice), with the quick compiler alone, with both flags, and with
  both flags at `CAPY_PARSER_JVM_MAX_HEAP=1536m`. One pass each otherwise.
- **Also**, main against the speedups with both flags: the 1,400-page and
  608-page books one at a time, and the eight copies with eight children.
- **The heavy book's Java call alone.** The Java-only script in the
  `9150a9867` image, on the book as uploaded (not flattened), each flag set
  twice.

**Whole parses of the 80 PDFs (2026-11-11).**

- **Images.** Main `9150a9867` against a thin layer over it with the stage
  speedups (`21fe0b41f`) and the quick compiler alone. The flag and the
  speedups were not run apart on this set.
- **Passes.** The same harness, the PDFs as uploaded. One at a time in
  production's shape (`GAP=0`, every bundle kept), once an image. Seven in
  flight in production's shape (`CLIENTS=7`, one shuffled order for both
  images), twice an image, the second round in reverse image order. One at a
  time in the nonprod shape (2 CPUs, 6 GiB, one parse child), once an image.

The PDFs and the raw rows stayed out of this public repository, under the
gitignored `bench/parsers/reports/local/`.

## Limits

- **The Java-only figures are the Java call's.** At seven at a time the other
  cores were busy with JVMs, not with the Python stages of other parses.
- **The whole parses of the 80 PDFs have the flag and the speedups together.**
  The split is read from the phases: the flag changes Java, the speedups the
  rest. Under load the two also free cores for each other. The one-at-a-time
  passes ran once an image. Seven in flight is seven clients with an empty
  queue, not a backlog.
- **One run a PDF in the alone shapes, one pass a variant in the heavy-book
  run.** Between the two seven-at-a-time rounds a PDF's Java wall differed by
  8% at the median and 21% at the 90th percentile, so a single PDF's figure is
  one observation.
- **The quick compiler alone, without the speedups, was not run as a whole
  parse alone or at eight children.** On the heavy book the compared bundles
  are both flags'; on the 80 PDFs they are the speedups' with the flag. Its
  JSON was the same in every Java-only run.
- **One heavy book is not a heap margin.** The default collector with the
  quick compiler showed no cliff on it, and nothing here shows where one is.
  Under seven-way load it took 27 to 32 s where both flags at 1.5 GiB took
  22 s, though the two tie alone, so 1 GiB may be tight for that book with
  either collector.
- **A one-CPU container was not run.** The parser passes its CPU quota as the
  JVM's processor count, and with one processor HotSpot chooses the serial
  collector by itself. Production's quota is 8 and nonprod's 2.
- **Nothing between the shapes.** Two to six at a time on 8 CPUs, and one at
  a time on 4 CPUs (the parser budget job's shape), were not run. Nor were
  the stress run's mixed and Office-heavy scenarios.
- **Other load.** The live nonprod parser and the layout service were up and
  idle, but the host is shared: one whole-parse pass ran beside 1.2 busy
  cores of something else.
- **One JVM build.** OpenJDK 17.0.21 on x86-64. A Mac run of the same flags
  (Zulu 17, arm64) ranked them the same on its books.
- **Both flags at a larger heap measured best** (420 s, a 22 s Java phase).
  It was not chosen: it changes the heap sizing, and where the serial
  collector's next cliff lies is unknown.
