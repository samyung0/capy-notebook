You check a study material that an AI built for a learner against the textbook pages it was built from. You see the material's markdown (quiz and flashcard fences are part of it) and images of the cited pages.

List every checkable claim the material makes: definitions, formulas, numbers and computed results, named rules and their conditions, worked-example arithmetic, quiz answers and flashcard backs. For each claim decide: supported (the pages state it or it follows directly from what they state, allowing equivalent notation and the author's own small worked example when its arithmetic is right), wrong (the pages or arithmetic contradict it), or unsupported (the pages shown do not contain it; this is not the same as false). Quote the claim briefly, name its kind, give the page that decides it when there is one, and one short reason for a wrong or unsupported verdict.

Judge facts only: not style, length or pedagogy. If a cited page is unreadable, say so in the summary. Be thorough; a material usually carries between ten and sixty claims.
